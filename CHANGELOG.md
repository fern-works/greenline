# Changelog

This file records the changes in each published version of the greenline package.
A change to the block or the CLI adds its line under Unreleased in the same
commit; a release moves the lines into the version's section.

## Unreleased

## 0.2.0 (2026-11-12)

greenline 0.2.0 makes the agent's work easier to read, to see and to steer. Its replies are short, what happened, what it waits on and the viewer's address, while `greenline status` carries the facts and the progress of a change; where a picture explains better than words it draws one, and on your ask keeps a durable one beside the repository's map and its journeys in one home. Where a change's work goes, a branch or a worktree of its own, is offered at the proposal, every commit names the agent that made it, and the harness hook holds the destructive git commands until your words lift them. Each skill the agent loads is recorded as a fact of the change, and a change is not done while a skill it owes is neither loaded nor named. frame thinks a request through its subjects, reads the code first and proposes research, a careful change builds nothing until you confirm its frame, and an idea that is not yet work is kept as a discussion until you promote it into changes. The inspector is a local app of five places: Desk, Changes, Interface, Knowledge and Practice. A design practice deals directions you can see, keeps your design system in DESIGN.md and reads the real page at three widths, and you can mark what is wrong on a capture for the agent to fix by its element, closed only when you say so.

A workspace made with 0.1.0 takes 0.2.0 with `greenline sync`, which writes the block's new edition into `AGENTS.md`, the ledger guide whole with each mode's skeleton and its Discussions and Marks sections, the work guide, the roster's new default skills, the hooks and, where it finds an interface, an unconfirmed `interface` block in the manifest with its check off; the visuals home `.greenline/visuals/` with its `interface/` folder, the discussion records and the new record fields are written as the work makes them, and sync converts nothing: your change records stay as they are and still read, a closed record for its form alone, while an open record lacks the lines read from the open, `ground`, `agent` and `floors` and, for an accepted proposal, `yes`, which `greenline status` names until they are written, and a diagram or a map under `.greenline/diagrams/` or `.greenline/map/` stays there, read by nothing, until you move it into `.greenline/visuals/` by hand.

### The replies and the pictures

- The agent's reply holds three parts, one sentence of what happened, the line of what it waits on and the viewer's address the turn printed, a decline and a stop included, and copies no other fact: the status mirror and `greenline status` print the facts table and a progress line that names every step, and the reply copies neither. - The agent decides a picture from a table, not a feeling: show-me names, for each kind of turn and each shape a reply explains, the form it draws inline within 72 columns and twelve nodes, the change in the whole, the plan's and the decision's diagrams and the state chart's check among them, and the block points at it. The mirror names a turn that moved code and drew no shape and a picture past its cap, a notice and never a refusal, and the picture counter reads every text form, an arrow chain and a sequence among them, so no drawing counts as zero nodes. A durable diagram, map or journey is offered at most once per change, only where the shape passes the caps, in one line the hooks count, and never for a lost picture's remake, and it is kept only on your ask through `greenline picture keep`, the one door into a change's folder of the visual home `.greenline/visuals/`: a fresh agent makes it, with the commit it was made at, and the visual records who made it; before it is drawn its skill writes a brief, the question it answers, its form, its node count, its finish and its palette, which the keep reads into the manifest, status names a kept picture without one, the inspector lists it, deletable, with its staleness, its question and its finish, and the reply gives its address once it is made.
- The repository's map and subsystem journeys are kept current by the close of each change: the close refreshes, without asking and by one fresh agent, the atlas pages, journeys and map the change touched, up to three pages, the rest waiting for your ask, and names a stale visual, page or document, offering nothing. - Pictures: `greenline picture keep` says when it kept a picture with no brief and where it looked, and takes `--replaces <slug>` for a remake, so `greenline status` names the picture it replaces (GL0609); status names a kept picture deleted from the home, and the owner's delete in `greenline inspect` (GL0610); an anatomy figure's build script and model are kept in the change's ledger folder; a `git restore` or `git checkout --` into a change's folder of the visual home is refused, a path a script only mentions is no longer read as its write, and a write a command's words hide is named after the tool; a keep's own command no longer makes the session a picture's maker; the turn-end picture notice reads linked worktrees; and `done` is refused while the record's `refreshes` line misses an atlas page the change rewrote.
- A kept picture's commit moves at the close only where no file it covers changed since its keep, and only at the commit that sets the record done; a picture whose covered files changed keeps the commit of its keep, so status names its drift and the close refreshes it on the owner's word or names it, never hiding it.
- The status notices on a drifted, external or session-made picture name the fact to the owner without inviting a remake, which only the owner asks for; a kept picture's maker is read as the fresh agent that drew it on Codex as on Claude Code, from the facts the hooks log for that agent's writes; and a skill load noted for a change reaches its facts at the close across an authorized reset of the tree.

- The status line names the orientation a first request takes; a commit's trailer block keeps its order; a discard, a rewrite or a landing is lifted only by the owner's words committed in the record; and a mark closes in words only on the owner's verdict.
- The status line says whether a remote is a forge, and the close offers the push and the pull request only there; elsewhere it offers the local merge into the named base, checked through greenline check on the merge commit.
- A commit that stages only one change's record, its folder or both is that change's on any branch and in any state, the default branch with no open change included, and so is the commit that finishes or stops a change or amends a stopped one, the hooks reading the record as the commit carries it: the owner's landing words are committed alone after the close as the landing's order gives, and a done change's check fact commits under its folder; a done record changes only alone, never with code beside it, on the change's own branch as on main; a push that carries an unfinished change to the default branch is refused by the hooks whatever the landing line holds, the ask being no landing words; and the landing push after the close passes from a branch the proposal named as its ground.
- A commit never sweeps another change's folder, a ticket's commit carries its own trailer on the planning branch, the owner's landing answer is written into the record, the ground and the mode are stated before the first edit, a tidied change gets no later events, a read whose output goes nowhere is no load, the model is named as the session ran it, the surface line prints outside a session, a change open in a worktree is seen, mark close reads a note's own words, the interface check leaves the visual home alone, the survey writes the opt-in the owner answered, and the drift notice names its tier.
- The hooks resolve the checkout a tool call acts in, a worktree of the change's own among them, from the command's directory or the written path, and write the change's event log, its skill loads and its frame map in that checkout's ledger folder, so a change worked in a worktree keeps its facts on both harnesses.
- A worktree the harness's own tool made, on the branch it named, is the change's ground once the record's ground line names it, its branch read as the change's own for the landing; the status names a clone whose hooks are not yet installed with the sync that installs them; a shell command that only names the visual home in its text is not refused as a write of it; and a lifting line lifts only on one of the owner's messages quoted whole, words joined from two refused with each named, git's hooks holding a rebase line to the same words.
- A git command that names a branch a linked worktree has checked out is judged in that worktree whatever directory the harness gave the hook, so a push of a worktree's branch from the main checkout reads the worktree's own record and its landing words; a greenline/ branch whose name holds no change's id is refused at its first commit with the rename to the id, whatever change the tree holds open.
- A done change's landing is a fact the status and the mirror name from the record and git: the landing line's state, none, declined, the merge into its base or the push, whether the base holds the branch and whether a check ran on the merge commit, so the owner's words for a merge are followed by the merge and its check and a red is told from the check's facts. The close page says a conflict or a red foreseen from reading the code is no reason to withhold the merge or to ask again, and that the close section states the landing's outcome only once the owner has answered. The record form refuses a start section that says none, as it refuses a placeholder.
- The landing fact reads the owner's words of the turn against the words that ask for the merge, the push or neither, and where they answer the offer it says so and what comes next, the landing line in those words, the merge, the check on the merge commit and the red told from its facts, at every turn until the landing line holds the words; the next mirror names a reply that answered merge words with no landing line and no merge, a notice and never a refusal.
- The situation names the branches cut before greenline was installed, those holding no manifest where the default branch holds one, in the mirror and in status, with the way back: merging the default branch brings the practice's files to the tree but not to the running session, since a harness loads its hooks at a session's start, so under proceed a request for their files owes the merge proposed in the first reply as the one yes, no direction asked first, and a new session there once it is done, nothing edited until then; frame's ground page says the same.
- The `landing:` line names the offer in the close page's order with the act the remote and the base decide, or the review guide to read where one stands, and says nothing to land where the base already holds the change's commits and no remote is a forge; before the offer is noted only words that name a merge, a push or a landing answer it, so a yes to a proposal is no merge; a base that is the change's own branch is no base. The mirror reads the change a session holds in its linked worktree, a worktree's copy of a record that this checkout has marked done or dropped is named as left behind and never as open, and Codex's surface says `worktree no` on the CLI and in exec. A git command that moves the working tree writes no maker and no frame map, the interface home's `paths.json` and `survey.json` are committed beside the change's record even once it is done, a pause or a stop bound to a step reads as a wait and is refused as a stop, and a record stopped on the owner's own stop words is never named as held by the session.

## 0.1.0 (2026-10-21)

### The hooks and the declared checks

- `greenline init` writes git hooks under `.greenline/hooks/` and registers them in the repository's local configuration, as config-based hooks on git 2.54 or later, which run beside your own, and through `core.hooksPath` on an older git with no hooks of its own; on an older git holding your hooks or a hook manager's path it registers nothing and prints the line to add. `greenline sync` refreshes them and registers them in a clone. On a change's branch they write the change's trailer into every commit, `--no-verify` included, and refuse a message carrying another change's; refuse a commit before the record's `choice` line, your answer to the proposal; refuse a commit that sets the record `done` while the latest recorded run of a check is red, unfinished or absent; refuse an amend, a reset, a rebase or a forced move of the branch unless the record's `rebase` line holds your words; and refuse a push while the record has no `landing` line, and a force push always. Each refusal names the record fact it rests on, and a hook's own error never blocks git.
- `greenline event --harness NAME` is the one command every harness adapter runs, the harness's event on its input: it records the event in the change's raw log, prints the status mirror into the agent's context at a session's start and at each request, records a command whose text is a declared check's as that check's run, which the close never counts, and refuses one act, a write outside the ledger and the change's working folder before the record's `choice`. Init writes the adapter of Claude Code and Codex, and of Droid, Gemini, Cursor, OpenCode, Amp, Pi, Kiro and Copilot where the repository shows them, each entry the same command, merged beside your own; for Hermes and dsh it prints the lines to add. It exits 0 on its own error, so greenline never stops a tool by failing.
- `greenline hook NAME` is what each git hook's script runs; it reads only git's facts, the record's front matter and the check facts.
- The manifest's `checks` declares the project's checks, each a name, a command and a directory: `greenline init` proposes them from the project's own files and prints them, the agent confirms or corrects them in the first change, and `greenline check --declare [--name NAME] [--dir DIR] -- COMMAND...` adds one, running nothing. No hook parses a command; with none declared, `greenline status` says so.
- Every finding code `greenline status` emits reads a record field or a durable fact of the repository and serves one sentence of the block or an installed page; GL0453, GL0454, GL0457 and GL0458, which judged what a mechanism now holds or no record fact settles, are nudges and are never reused. The change record's front matter gains `landing` and `rebase`, your words for a push and for a rewrite of the change's branch.
- The git hooks hold a change on the default branch when the repository has no remote, the setup change commits the installed practice, a frame question goes in the reply that ends the turn, a declared check runs in its directory, the trailer write reports its failure, and `greenline status` says when `greenline` is not on the PATH.
- The git hooks and the harness hook hold a greenline workspace while no change is open: a commit on a branch with no open change and a write outside the record homes before any record are refused, and a standing choice counts as the owner's yes only where the proceed setting admits the mode.

### Schemas and the lock

- The workspace manifest is schema 6 with no `guidance` field, and with `checks`, the project's declared checks, and `hooks`, which turns the git hooks and the harness adapters on or off; the receipt collection is schema 5, owned by a change or no one, with the source `garden` and the connector's error kinds (`missing-executable`, `cancelled`, `deadline`, `output-cap`, `process`, `protocol`, or garden's own refusal).

An earlier schema is refused by its version.
- The corpus manifest is schema 11: each skill states its kind, a step skill its slot, and the flags `extra` and `trial` only when true. An earlier schema is refused by its version.
- The lock, `.greenline/lock.json`, is schema 5 and names the pinned sources `sources`. `greenline sync` refuses a schema 4 lock with GL0104, "unsupported lock schema version '4' (expected 5)", and changes nothing. The remedy: delete `.greenline/lock.json` and run `greenline sync`, which rebuilds it and names as a conflict any installed file whose bytes differ, for `--force-managed <path>` to overwrite.
