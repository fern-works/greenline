# Close: the practice's mechanics

This page holds close at each mode's depth and where its work lands: the section, the record's state and cost, the commands the close and its audit run, the stops, the landing and the decisions. Read it with `SKILL.md` when the mode reaches close, and when AGENTS.md's index sends you here.

## The depths

- **Inline, at quick.** Close the record by `SKILL.md`'s steps, every one of them, the checks' last run and `greenline tidy` included. The change record's close section, in `.greenline/ledger/<id>.md`, is one line: each ADR written, by its path, and each term written down, or none, the landing done, and the audit's result, the marks line, where it is owed, as its last clause or on a line of its own.
- **Written, at regular and careful.** Close the record by `SKILL.md`'s steps. The close section holds the decisions recorded, each ADR by its path or the statement that none was needed, the terms written down, the landing offered and what was done, and the audit's result: each gap it found and how the record was corrected.

## The record's marks and the commands

Close reads the change's working files under `.greenline/tmp/<id>/`. The checks it records again, after the decisions and the terms, run through `greenline check`: a write outside `.greenline/` moves the code a check last ran on, and `greenline status --change <id>` names each such check (GL0451). After a local merge, the change's checks run on the merged result through `greenline check`. `greenline status --change <id>` lists the change's commits, found by their trailer `Greenline-Change: <id>`, its checks under `.greenline/ledger/<id>/checks/`, the files it changed and each brief as `greenline brief` wrote it, and counts the commits since the last recorded check; the audit and the close section read their numbers there.

The close sets the front matter: the state `done`, or `dropped` for a change the owner dropped; the "next" line `none`; and the cost, the agents and the rounds the change used, with `starts` naming the starts of the line's steps alone, such as `review 1`, a restart one more line, `review 1 restarted`, and one more agent; a method's agent, a picture's helper among them, is named in the section it serves and listed nowhere. Write the cost as it ran, never lowered to fit the mode: where it passes the mode's count and the owner accepted that in words, quote them in the `overrun` line beside it, `overrun: "<the owner's words>"`, and `greenline status` reads the cost as accepted. Write the `outcome` line, `outcome: "<what landed>"`, one sentence in your own words under 200 characters saying what landed, never the why repeated: the owner's view of the change opens on it, and git's hooks refuse `done` without it or over the length. Write `skills`, the skills the change used, by name, in your own words for what it followed; `greenline status --change <id>` lists the skill loads the hooks noted beside it, a hint to reconcile and never a line written for you, and the close section records them beside the skills the sections name. Set `done` only once the record holds every line its mode owes and no placeholder, which `greenline status` names as `record lacks:`, and once it loaded each skill the `owes:` line named or the close section quotes the owner's waiver; git's hooks refuse `done` otherwise. Set `done` only once the record holds its forms: each frame question answered or marked `answer: open`, each section at the independent or looped depth citing its own reports alone and as many as its depth starts, and a plan the change extended committed with the close; git's hooks refuse `done` otherwise, naming the first breach. Set `done` only once `greenline status --change <id>` reports no cost below the starts the record names (GL0409). Set `done` only when the latest run of every check under `.greenline/ledger/<id>/checks/` is green, as `greenline status --change <id>` shows; `SKILL.md`'s step 7 says what a red one asks. Remove the working files with `greenline tidy --change <id>` at every close, the quick mode's inline close included: it removes `.greenline/tmp/<id>/` once the state is `done` or `dropped`, and refuses while a record links to a file in it. The close leaves no working directory of a done change: where `greenline status` still names one, such as a test's scratch written under an earlier change's id, move what the test writes under this change's folder and run `greenline tidy`, which removes every done change's working files. A lasting choice is linked from the decisions book, `.greenline/DECISIONS.md`. Every commit the close and a stop make takes a subject in the form build's commit page gives, `<type>(<scope>): <imperative summary>`, unless the repository keeps a convention of its own.

The audit's parts, each written in the close section: the skills the sections and the `skills` line name against the loads `greenline status --change <id>` lists, each difference named, declared and never loaded or loaded and not declared; each lasting choice by its entry in the decisions book, or one sentence of why none is owed, never "No ADR needed" alone; at careful, the review section's one table, a row for every report, one that found nothing included; and the checks run again wherever the status counts a commit since the last run.

The skeleton of the record for each mode, its front matter and its sections, is in the workspace's ledger guide, `.greenline/ledger/README.md`; close fills it and does not restate it.

## Stops

A wait for the owner within the session, at a proposal, a question or a review, is no stop: the record stays `open`, and where a record reads `stopped` while the session that wrote it goes on, the status line says so and you set it `open` again. A pause or a stop bound to a step, such as "pause after the build", is such a wait: the record stays `open`, and the hooks refuse a `stopped` line that quotes only those words. A stop is the owner's words that end the work for now, such as "stop there for today": set the state `stopped`, quote those words whole, one message of theirs, in its `stopped` line, rewrite the "next" line and commit the record and its folder, the check facts under it, and nothing else; working edits the owner asks to commit are a commit of their own, apart from the stop's. Make the stop's commit in the stop's own turn: after the write that sets `stopped`, the hooks say `stopped: <record> reads stopped and no commit holds it`, and the status line says so until the commit stands, since a clone and a fresh session carry commits alone. A record stopped on the owner's own stop words is the owner's stop, and the status line never names it as held by the session. The hooks refuse the commit that sets `stopped` with no `stopped` line, on words no message of the owner's holds or that read as no stop, and on the words of an earlier stop. A request for another change stops the change this session holds first, its state `stopped`, its `stopped` line `"another change: <the owner's request>"` and its "next" line, and then gets its own proposal. A stopped change is set `open` before any edit of its record or its code: a session that resumes one, which `router` finds, does so as its first write and writes on in its record; where its first write leaves the record `stopped`, the hooks say so, and you set it `open`. A "next" line that waits on the owner, such as the owner's review of the tests, is the resume's first stop: the status line prints it as `waiting on:`, and the work goes no further than that wait until the owner answers it. The owner's words that answer it, such as "the tests look right; write the code", lift it: set the record `open` as your first write, rewrite the "next" line and go on from the step it names; a resumed change never refuses the owner's permission to continue. A resume, such as "carry on with that change", answers the stop and not a wait the "next" line names for the owner's review or answer: where the status line says `the wait on the owner stands`, set the record `open` as your first write, do the wait's act first, showing again what it waits on where this checkout holds none, and wait for the owner's answer to it. The owner's words of the turn outrank the record's next line and any earlier words to pause again: where the status line says `the owner's words this turn answer it`, the resumed change sets the record `open` as its first write and goes on from the wait the stop interrupted. Where the tree holds uncommitted edits at the stop, such as tests you showed the owner, the hooks name them: keep them as `shown.patch` in the record's folder, `git diff HEAD -- <their paths>`, so the stop's commit carries them, since a clone carries commits alone; in a clone the status line names the patch to apply before the wait's act, and a later write may remove it. The stop's commit carries the change's trailer, and the working files stay for the close that finishes the change, which `SKILL.md`'s closing order states. A stop's reply says what stopped and the step reached in one sentence, and what the owner's next word decides on its `Waiting on:` line, as AGENTS.md's reply section gives it. A change stopped in a worktree of its own commits there and its worktree stays: `greenline status` in the main checkout names it with the worktree's path, and the session that resumes it works there.

## The landing

The landing is offered as `SKILL.md`'s closing order says, and the close section records what was done. Every close, the quick mode's included, writes its terms and decisions before the checks' last run, so no check is older than the code, and ends with `greenline tidy --change <id>`. The interface home's `paths.json` and `survey.json` under `.greenline/ledger/interface/`, which the change's interface check writes, go in the commit of the record or its folder, the close's or a later one: git's hooks admit them there, `done` or not, and refuse them beside code once the record is `done`. The close's reply holds AGENTS.md's three parts: one sentence of what landed, the line of what the owner's next word decides and the viewer's address. What was checked, and what was not under `Not probed`, `Not landed` and `Not mine`, a limit naming what was not run, never an environment fact not checked, stand in the close section. It counts no commits and gives no reasons for them, notes no reopen, trailer or sessions folder and does no cost arithmetic.

A forge's landing is one pull request per change, and one per planning change, its tickets' commits grouped in it; its title and its body are written as `pr-writing` says, the body linking the record. A change over about 500 changed lines, as `git diff --shortstat <base>...HEAD` counts them, says so in the body and the reply, with the split the plan should have made; the size is a note, never a gate. The owner's words for the push and the pull request go in the record's `landing` line, and the record is committed with them, before either: git's hooks refuse a push of the change's branch without the committed line, and the harness hook a push to another branch. The owner's ask to land is not those words unless it says so of the push or the merge. An ask to push an unfinished change to the default branch is not those words either: decline once, naming what the push skips, the review, the verify and the close, and ask for the close first; git's hooks and the harness hook refuse a push of the change to the default branch until the record is `done` in a commit, whatever its `landing` line holds.

The offer reads the remote's kind from the status line, `remote: origin, a forge (github.com)`, `remote: origin, not a forge (a local path)` or `remote: none`, never from the URL: the push and the pull request only where it names a forge, and the local merge where it names no forge or none, an unknown host among them, the owner free to answer with the push instead. While the offer waits for the owner's word the record stays open, since the wait is no stop. The close section states the landing's outcome only once the owner has answered: until then it states the offer in its words, and the turn that receives the owner's answer writes the `landing` line and the close section together. An answer with no act, the branch left, the merge declined or later, is written into the `landing` line as `"declined: <the owner's words>"`, which lifts no push or merge, and the close section names it, so "landing offered, not done" never stands alone once the owner has answered; commit the record and its folder alone, as for the words of an act. Once the close has run, the status line's `landing:` names the landing's state from the record and git: no landing recorded and whether the base holds the branch, the merge owed on the owner's words, or the merge checked at its commit. Until the owner answers, the same line names the offer in this page's order and, after `the facts decide:`, the act the remote and the base decide, or, where a contributing guide stands, its path and this section; the line makes no recommendation, so recommend the choice in your reply, as `SKILL.md`'s closing order says, from those facts or from the guide's review rule; it says `nothing to land: the commits are on <branch>` where the base already holds the change's commits and no remote is a forge, and then no words answer it. While no `landing` line stands, the same line says when the owner's words since the offer ask for the merge, the push or neither, and what comes next, until the `landing` line holds those words, and the next turn names a reply that merged nothing on their merge words. Before the offer is noted, only words that name the merge, the push or the landing answer it, so a yes to a proposal or a review is no merge. The owner's words for a merge are acted on in the turn they come, in the order below: whether to merge is the owner's word, so a conflict or a red foreseen from reading the code, a `git show` of the base's new commit among them, is no reason to withhold the merge or to ask again. The merge and its check on the merge commit come first, and a red is told from the check's facts, which `greenline status --change <id>` names (GL0404). A local merge runs in this order on the owner's words:

1. On the change's branch, write the owner's words in the record's `landing` line after the base the offer named, `landing: "merge into <base>: <their words>"`, since git's hooks refuse a merge into a base the line does not name; the merge into the base and its check on the merge commit in the close section, and the state as `SKILL.md`'s step 7 sets it; commit the record and its folder there, before the merge. Where the close committed the record `done` before the owner's words came, write them after it and commit the record and its folder alone the same way: git's hooks take that commit as the change's in every state, and a `done` record never goes with code beside it.
2. Write the merge's message to `.greenline/tmp/<id>/merge-message.txt`: its subject `Merge <id> into <base>`, or `Merge <id>` where that passes 72 characters, a plan's landing taking the planning change's id, and its last paragraph the change's trailers, `Greenline-Agent: <agent>` where the record states its `agent` line, then `Greenline-Change: <id>`. Check out the base and merge with its commit, `git merge --no-ff -F .greenline/tmp/<id>/merge-message.txt <branch>`; never `--no-commit` or `--squash`, and never `-F -`, since git reads no message from stdin.
3. Run each check of the change on the merge commit through `greenline check --change <id> --step close -- <command>`, so its facts name the merge; it is the change's last check.
4. Commit those facts on the base, the record and its folder alone: git's hooks take the commit after the landing's merge as the change's and refuse its `done` while the latest run of a check, the merge's among them, is red. On a red result the same commit sets the state back to `open`, its "next" line naming the red check, and the close section says the merge stands red.
5. Commit nothing else on the base: the hooks refuse every other commit there while no change is open on it.

A merge that fails, on a conflict or a command's error, is reported as it stands with git's words and left for the owner, never checked by another runner: no probe of a would-be merge, a `git merge-tree` tree or a scratch copy, stands for the check on the merge commit. Once the owner has concluded or aborted it, the record goes back to `open` on the change's branch, its "next" line naming the merge, and its close section says the merge failed. A hook's refusal is answered by the line it names, never by `--no-verify`, which is the owner's bypass alone. A refusal you believe wrong is reported to the owner by its first sentence, in their terms, and never worked around, by a working copy set apart from the index or any other way.

The commits are regrouped before the pull request only where the owner asks and the record's `rebase` line holds the owner's words for it, committed before the rewrite, as [history.md](history.md) says, the tree compared before and after with `git rev-parse HEAD^{tree}`. A force push of a branch already pushed needs both the `rebase` and the `landing` lines, and a rejected push is reported, never forced without them. A slice found broken at close is fixed forward with a new commit; throwing work away is the owner's word in the record's `discard` line.

The branch and the worktree are cleaned up only on the owner's word, offered in the landing's line once it is done: the branch deleted with `git branch -d greenline/<id>` once it is merged, and `git branch -D` only under the `discard` or `landing` line; a worktree of the change's own removed with `greenline tidy --change <id> --worktree`, run from the main checkout once the change is done there, a worktree that kept an open copy of the record after the merge among them, which refuses a worktree holding uncommitted work.

## The visuals

The close's checklist holds the visuals the change made or touched. A visual the change made stands under `.greenline/visuals/<id>/` with its manifest, kept there by `greenline picture keep` on the owner's ask, and the record links them once, as `[visuals](<id>/visuals.json)`; at the write that sets the record done the hooks move a manifest to the commit HEAD names only where no file it covers changed since its own commit, and the closing commit carries the moved ones; a picture whose covered files changed keeps its commit, and status names its drift. The close refreshes, without asking, the atlas pages and journeys whose covered files the change touched, and the map when a page changed, up to three pages: one fresh agent, started with the `explain` start's brief, re-reads only the changed files, and the record's `refreshes` line names the pages it rewrote and its cost, the agent named apart and counted nowhere. The status line's `atlas:` line names those pages at the close, and done waits for them unless the owner's words "leave the pages" stand in the close section. A page, journey or the map a refresh rewrote is named in that line whatever the owner's words, and done waits until it is; the `atlas:` line names one the line misses. Past three pages the rest wait for the owner's ask. The map's first pages, an anatomy figure and a document the owner wrote are made or rewritten only on the owner's ask. A stale visual, page or document the status lines name is named in the close section, and nothing is offered.

## Decisions

A repository's existing home for a kind of memory is that home; a default home AGENTS.md or `.greenline/WORK.md` names is created only where the repository has none. The repository's law file changes, and an existing decision is superseded, only on the owner's request; a new reversible entry in the decision home is yours to add. A product behavior the request left open (what a user would notice) and the frame does not ask is decided, recorded as reversible in the repository's decision home (the book only where the repository has none) and named in the reply in one line; a product's name, what ships first, a paid service or a consequential exception is asked. A decision whose options differ in shape is drawn inline where its shape decides, and `diagram-design` keeps its diagram on the owner's ask.

## The interface home

On a change whose record says `interface: yes`, close updates the home with what the change settled, DESIGN.md's tokens and sections and the `## Interface` rulings of `.greenline/DECISIONS.md`, in the change's last commit; and a pull request's body shows the captures before and after, from `.greenline/visuals/<id>/`.

## Marks

The close's checklist holds the change's marks, which `greenline mark list --all` prints. Each mark the change answered is proposed closed with its evidence, the capture after and the commit, as design's marks page says, before the close is offered. Set the record done only when every mark of the change is addressed or declined by the owner's act, or the owner's word lets one stay open, which the close section quotes; a closure in words quotes the owner's verdict on that mark, and an instruction to close marks unseen is not one, so they stay open and the reply says so; a mark that stays open stays in the change's folder, and the status line keeps printing it. The close section's marks line is the `marks:` line `greenline status` prints, copied as it stands, or `marks: none open` where it prints none, never written from memory, on a line of its own or, in a quick close's one line, as its last clause, since the clause runs to the line's end: git's hooks refuse `done` while it disagrees with the marks files, and while the record's `## Interface` section names a floor finding with no ruling of the owner's.
