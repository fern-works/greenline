# Frame: the practice's mechanics

This page holds the frame step at each mode's depth and where its work lands, with what comes before it: the orientation, reading the situation, a question, the proposal, where the commits go and the record's opening. Read it with `SKILL.md` when the mode reaches frame, and when AGENTS.md's index sends you here.

## The depths

- **Inline, at quick.** Run `SKILL.md`'s method for the intent and the one open question. The change record's frame section, in `.greenline/ledger/<id>.md`, is that one line: the intent, then the open question with its answer, or a start's answers, or none. The line credits the owner only with a choice the owner made, in the request or in an answer; a choice you made is written as yours. It also names each floor found and where it came from, and under a mode the owner chose below one, the floor's reason.
- **Written, at regular.** Read the repository's documents first, as `SKILL.md` says, and write the frame without an interview round with the owner: the section restates the ask, states the acceptance, names every floor found with where it came from, and lists the open questions the request leaves, each answered from the repository and the request with the answer taken, so the owner can correct it on the proposal. The questions take the list form below, each `decided: agent`.
- **Written, at careful, and when frame is asked for by name.** Read the repository's documents, then run the interview in [interview.md](interview.md) to its end. At this depth every behaviour the request leaves open goes to the owner through the interview, each with your recommended answer, and none is settled on your own. A fact the repository's documents or code settle is no question: write it in the frame with its source, never as a round the owner answered. A question to the owner goes through the channel [interview.md](interview.md) names, the harness's question tool where the status line says `questions cards` and the reply that ends your turn otherwise, and you wait for the answer; never put it through a side channel the owner may not see, such as an input call a headless session hides. A behaviour the scope the owner accepted already settles is yours: decide it within that scope and name it in the frame section and the reply as your choice; one it leaves open still goes to the owner. The frame section holds the interview's questions in the list form below, each with its answer and who decided, and the nodes left closed with why; then the confirmed restatement with its out-of-scope list, the terms the interview settled, the floors found with where each came from, and any question the owner chose to leave open, its answer `open`. At careful the section covers every node but `generic`, each asked by a question that names it or closed by a line that names it with your reason, and holds the restatement outside the list: `greenline status` names a node neither asked nor closed, or a missing restatement, while the record is open, and git's hooks refuse `done` over either. Once the frame is whole, put the restatement to the owner, its out-of-scope line with it, by a card where the status line says `questions cards` and in the reply that ends your turn otherwise; write the next line `frame: wait for the owner's confirmation of the frame's restatement`, and wait. The hooks note when the restatement was put. Quote the owner's words that confirm it in the record's `confirmed` line, as they said them: a yes or plain words such as "That's correct." or "Exactly." in the message's opening clause, with no question and no word of negation or contrast such as "not", "dont", "but" or "except", written with an apostrophe or without, since such a message confirms nothing and the restatement is put again. Only words said after it count, never the yes to the proposal or an interview's answer, and a refinement or a no revises the frame, which you put again. Until those words stand, the harness's before-write hook and git's pre-commit refuse every write and commit outside the change's homes, `.greenline/ledger/` and `.greenline/tmp/<id>/`, each refusal naming what is missing; a commit of the record and its folder alone goes through. This holds under `proceed`, which admits the mode and confirms no frame, and from a raise to careful on, on whatever ground the change takes; a record that does not parse holds the doors until it does, and a record set `dropped` before its confirmation holds them too, so commit it and its folder alone and make other work a new change. A ticket's frame is its planning change's, the record that names its plan: its writes and commits wait on that record's confirmation, a careful ticket whose plan no record names is held until one does, a dropped planning record that holds no confirmation still holds its tickets, and the ticket's own record holds no `confirmed` line.

## The question list form

A written frame section records its questions in one list form, from which `greenline event` derives the change's frame map, `.greenline/ledger/<id>/frame-map.json`, after each tool, until the commit that closes the record, so the map follows the frame's last answers. The map is generated and never edited, and the inspector draws it on the change page. Each round of the interview opens with a `### Round <n>` line; a section with none is round 1. Each question is one item, `- Q<n> [<node>] <the question>`, its node one of `SKILL.md`'s thirteen, with its lines indented beneath it:

```markdown
### Round 1

- Q1 [scope] Does the export include archived rows?
  - proposed: no, live rows only
  - answer: no, live rows only
  - decided: owner
  - research: [the encodings](<id>/frame-research-1.md)
- closed [security]: the rows hold no personal data
```

`proposed` is the answer you recommended and `answer` the one taken, `answer: open` where the owner chose to leave the question open, with `decided: owner`. `decided` is `owner` for the owner's answer and `agent` for a choice you made inside the scope the owner accepted, one word and never a sentence. Every question holds both before the record is set done: git's hooks refuse `done` while one lacks them. `research` links each report the question drew on. Each node left closed is one item, `- closed [<node>]: <why>`, and one item may close several nodes that share its reason, `- closed [<node>, <node>]: <why>`. Each research topic you propose stands beside the questions as one item, `- R<n> [<node>] <the topic>`, with `source:`, `question:` and `changes:` lines beneath it, and `accepted: "<the owner's words>"` once the owner accepts it. The restatement and the rest of the section stand outside the list.

## Where research and prototypes land

A fresh agent that does the research page's reading works by the start `research` and gets the one brief every fresh agent gets (`.greenline/ledger/README.md`, "The brief and the relay note"), its topic as the brief's extra. It writes its report under `.greenline/tmp/<id>/` and files it with `greenline research report <file> --change <id> --topic R<n>`, which writes `.greenline/ledger/<id>/frame-research-<n>.md` for the topic, its first line `<!-- generated by the research start: report <n> -->`, and refuses a topic the owner has not accepted; git's hooks refuse done while an accepted topic's report is not linked; the frame section cites it in the `research` line of each question it informs, and the frame map hangs it off them. The change's folder is `.greenline/ledger/<id>/`, its working files `.greenline/tmp/<id>/`, and a plan directory, whose research and prototypes the plan step writes, `.greenline/plans/<NNN-name>/`. A prototype, a probe and every other scratch file of the change go in its own working files, `.greenline/tmp/<id>/` under the id of its record, never in a folder under another id.

## The orientation

With no change record on any branch, local or remote-tracking, the first request's own change carries the orientation, which the `orientation` line of `greenline status` names, as a habit of its frame: it takes no record, no proposal and no wait of its own, and it is never a question put before the request's own proposal, so a request that names its mode, or that `proceed` allows, starts at once with the orientation inside it. Read the top-level documents and the code the request touches before that change's proposal, and write what you learned into the knowledge homes `.greenline/WORK.md` names as part of the change, before its close. On an empty tree (commits `none`, and no language and no document found) the orientation is `groundwork`: the setup is that first change's own work, built before the feature it asks for. A tree of documents without code, `survey (documents, no code)`, is no empty tree: read its documents before any plan. A survey, `architecture-survey`, runs only when the request asks for one or the owner names it. Outside a Git repository, offer `git init` in one line and end your turn: nothing is initialized or installed before the owner's yes. Load a start only when the request begins in its row's situation in AGENTS.md, or when the owner names it: it runs until it hands the job to a mode.

## Reading the situation

Find the intent, the current work, the authority and the affected roots in the repository's evidence: the request, the open and stopped change records under `.greenline/ledger/`, the open and parked discussions under `.greenline/ledger/discussions/`, the repository's own instructions (its contributing or law file and the instructions outside the managed markers of AGENTS.md) and its decision home. Before your first proposal in a session, read the situation line `greenline status` prints. Read the decisions book, `.greenline/DECISIONS.md`, when it exists. A path's nesting neither inherits nor overrules a decision; sibling roots stay independent. Lost context requires rereading the material the work needs, not crediting a compaction summary as a full read.

## Questions and named steps

A question needs no mode and no record, and neither does a step asked for by name that changes no file ("review this branch", "verify this", "grill me"), or a start that edits no file. A decision or a term written only into the knowledge homes (the decisions book, the ADR home, the glossary) opens a quick record first, as every write does. A factual question about this repository is answered from its files, as AGENTS.md's loop says. A follow-up inside an open change joins that change.

## The proposal

Before any edit, propose, in the reply that ends your turn. Open with one sentence of what would be built, then the change's id, the mode you would use from the table in AGENTS.md with its cost, where it goes and a floor only where you found one, and end on the line of what the owner's next word decides, `Waiting on:`, as AGENTS.md's reply section gives it; the proposal carries no facts table. Then the open points, each with the answer you would take, so a yes takes them all; one sentence of cost only where the mode is not quick; then "Yes?". A plain change's proposal stays under 120 words. The mode's reason is one clause. Read each floor in the code the request touches yourself, not only in the situation line: a secret, a key or a signature's expiry, an auth check, a migration, a public contract or a behaviour no check could fail; each raises the mode you propose and stands in the proposal with the file it came from. A request that touches a page or a screen states the `interface` fact in the proposal, and where `.greenline/manifest.json` holds no `interface` block, the same proposal offers the interface setting in the owner's words, as design's references page says, before any build. Whether this session can start a second agent is your own reading, never put to the owner: it can when the harness exposes a delegation tool in this session, and you never start one to find out. Say nothing the hooks already hold; the worktree is offered once a session, in prose, where the ground line offers it, and a side defect you found is named once, in one line. The owner accepts it or names another mode, branch or ground, and that choice is final; a mode named above yours has its cost restated before the work. A request that names a mode, a branch or a worktree has chosen it. From then on the mode is the one the status line prints, the owner's where they named another, never the mode you proposed. Proceed without waiting in two cases only: the request names the mode, or the workspace's `proceed` setting allows the proposed mode (none by default). Then the first reply states the mode taken and why, such as "the request names careful" or "`proceed` allows regular", with its floors and the ground, in one line before the first edit, and goes on: no orientation question, no survey proposal and no yes awaited. The owner's yes to a mode is the record's choice line, `owner` or `accepted`, written after the proposal: a request to commit, to checkpoint, to "go ahead" or to put a fix straight into a file is not that yes, and neither are words saying to implement without asking, which start the work only where `proceed` allows the mode: where a floor raises it above that, the proposal names the floor and asks that one yes. When you write `choice: accepted`, quote the owner's words that answered the proposal in the record's `yes` line, as they said them; where the hooks journaled the owner's messages and none of them holds those words as a yes, the hooks hold the work outside the homes and the first commit until the owner says yes; once that commit carries the line, the yes is settled, and a resume in a fresh session, a clone or a worktree asks no second yes. A `choice: standing` applies only where `proceed` admits the mode, that mode and every faster one; elsewhere the hooks refuse it. The proposal still comes before the first edit, the mode stated as taken with its reason and the ground, and asks only a substantive choice the request leaves open. The proposal, and any question with it, goes in the reply that ends your turn, never through a side channel the owner may not see.

Raising the mode during the work is a new proposal and waits the same way. Under a mode the owner named, a floor or a risk found later is reported and changes nothing; under a proposal the owner accepted, propose the raise it calls for. Either way the record names every floor found, as the ledger guide says: its `floors` line is owed from the open, a list of the floors found or `none`, done is refused without it, and the situation line names a floor the hooks saw that the line leaves out, for you to add where you judge it one. While the tree holds another change's uncommitted edits, propose no new change in this checkout: say so, recommend a worktree of its own, below, and wait for the owner, whatever `proceed` or the request allows. A change's id is chosen at its proposal, or earlier for a start's working file. A discussion, open or parked, that covers the request is named in the proposal with its idea and its state, and the owner's word decides whether the change is promoted from it, as "Discussions" below says.

## The ground

The ground is where the change's work and its commits go: a branch in this checkout, the current one or one of its own, or a worktree of its own, a second checkout of the repository on the change's own branch. It is read from local facts only, the current branch, the default branch, the remotes, a pull-request template and a CI workflow, with no network command: read the remote's state only as the repository holds it, its remote list, `refs/remotes/<remote>/HEAD` and the remote-tracking branches beside it, and the current branch's upstream, and before the proposal never run `git ls-remote`, `git fetch`, `git pull` or any other command that contacts a remote.

Where the repository has a remote and HEAD names its default branch, the proposal offers the ground two ways and recommends one. The branch of its own is recommended: a new branch, `greenline/<id>`, made from the default branch in this checkout. The worktree of its own on that branch is named as the one alternative, once a session, in one sentence of prose and never in a table's cell. A plain yes takes the branch, and the word worktree takes the worktree. Recommend the worktree first, the branch named as its alternative, only when this checkout holds unfinished work, another change is open here, or more than one agent works in the repository. Where the manifest's `ground` is `branch` or `worktree`, the owner's standing choice, which `greenline status` prints, state that ground as taken instead of offering the two, as `proceed` stands for the mode. Elsewhere the local facts give the branch and no offer is made: it is the current branch when that is not the default branch or the repository has no remote. The owner may still name the other ground, and the owner's word is final.

On a detached HEAD, name no branch and ask. The default branch is `origin/HEAD`, else `init.defaultBranch`, else the branch HEAD names unless it is a change's branch, as the ledger guide defines one, and with none, name no branch and ask; on another change's branch, never that branch: the default branch where there is no remote, and otherwise one made from it, `greenline/<id>`. A repository rule pulling the other way, in a contribution guide or a branch policy, is named beside the ground the local facts give, never in its place. In a repository with no remote, the git hooks hold the change whose record is open on the current branch, the default one included, as on a branch of its own: its trailer on every commit, no commit before its `choice` line. Where no change is open on the branch HEAD names, as on the default branch of a repository with a remote, which is never a change's branch, the hooks hold the workspace: they refuse a commit there, a local landing's close aside, and a write outside `.greenline/ledger/` and `.greenline/tmp/`.

Where a repository rule forbids committing on the branch the local facts give and the request names no branch, ask which, recommending `greenline/<id>`, and start once it is answered; a branch the owner leaves to you is one the repository's rules allow, never one they forbid.

On a branch cut before the install, which holds no `.greenline/manifest.json` where the default branch holds one, `greenline status` says the branch predates the practice and names the way back: switch to the default branch, or merge it into the branch. A harness loads its hooks at a session's start, so a session started on that branch loaded none, and the switch or the merge brings the practice's files to the tree but not to that session: once it is done, ask the owner to start a new session, and edit nothing until then. A hook that prints the same line at a turn's start runs in this session, so there the work goes on once the practice stands. The situation's `branches before the practice` line names such branches from any branch, at a session's and a turn's start and in `greenline status`: where the request names files only such a branch holds, propose the merge of the default branch into it as the one yes in the first reply, ask no direction first, and once it is done ask the owner for a new session there.

A worktree of its own is made as [worktree.md](worktree.md) says, once the owner has answered: with the harness's own tool where the status line's surface says `worktree yes`, otherwise with git, at `.worktrees/<id>` where the repository names no directory for worktrees, that directory ignored through `.git/info/exclude` and never by a commit. The record opens in it, and every command of the change runs there from then on; the project's test command, run there through `greenline check -- <command>` before the first edit of code, is its baseline. Where the harness or a sandbox refuses the worktree, say so, offer the branch of its own in place and wait for the owner's word. The worktree is the change's one checkout: a fresh agent the line starts works in it, never in a worktree of its own.

## Discussions

A discussion is an idea explored before it is work, beside a change or in place of one; the ledger guide's "Discussions" gives its record. Open one when you read the request as a whether, a what or a how about something not yet built, or when the owner asks for one: choose its id, a real date and a slug no change holds, and write `.greenline/ledger/discussions/<id>.md` with its `idea` and `state: open`. It needs no mode, no proposal, no branch and no yes. Run `SKILL.md`'s method at the written depth, the interview in [interview.md](interview.md) to its end where the owner takes part, its questions in the list form above under its `## frame`; research the owner accepts is read as [research.md](research.md) says, its report `.greenline/ledger/discussions/<id>/frame-research-<n>.md` and its scratch under `.greenline/tmp/<id>/`; each question still open stands under `## open questions`. It writes nothing outside its record, its folder and its working files: no code, no change record, no check. Commit the record and its folder in a commit of their own, where the owner sits; the hooks write its trailer.

At the end of a discussion session, ask the owner what becomes of it, recommending one: kept open; parked, with when to come back to it; declined, with the reason; promoted to one or more changes; or merged into another discussion. Set the state only on the owner's answer, quoting it in the `words` line; never on your own reading, and never moved without new words.

A discussion is promoted when the owner says which change or changes it becomes: a small one to one change, a large one to several, or to a planning change whose plan splits it into tickets. Each change then takes its own proposal, which states that it comes from the discussion, and on the owner's yes its record opens with `from: <id>`, a list where it draws on several discussions. Its frame section starts from the discussion's decisions, the questions it answered, restated with their answers as decided and asked of no one again; only what the discussion left open is asked. Copy the discussion's research reports into the change's folder, under `.greenline/ledger/<change>/discussions/<id>/`, and cite the copies, so the change's record stands on its own and two discussions' reports never meet. Then set the discussion `promoted`, its changes in `changes`, on the owner's words: its `changes` line names each change whose `from` line names it, and `greenline status` names a record whose discussion does not name it (GL0482).

## Opening the record

Before the first edit, switch to the ground the proposal named: the branch, creating it when it is new, or the worktree, made as above. Run `greenline open --change <id> --mode <mode>` there, which writes `.greenline/ledger/<id>.md` from the skeleton for its mode in `.greenline/ledger/README.md`, every line the mode owes named with a placeholder, and replace each placeholder in your own words: the ledger guide says when it is `open` or `stopped`; write its `floors` line, each floor the proposal named as a list or `none`; its `ground` line, `branch` or `"worktree: <path>"`, the worktree's path from the repository's root, and its `agent` line, `"<harness>/<model>"`: the harness as the status line's ground line names it and the model as the harness states it to you, never guessed and never a family name alone: where the status line's ground line names the model the harness reports, write that one. Every mode keeps the whole record: a step the mode does not run holds the one line `none at <mode>`, written as the record opens. One session writes the record at a time, the one holding the change. The change's first commit carries its record: `greenline status` reads history only from the commit where the first change record entered.

## Authority

Instructions and standing grants, explicit repository choices and actual platform constraints govern. Conventions are evidence; a consultation informs a justified gap and settles nothing the repository decided. Source prestige grants no authority. Surface a real conflict with its evidence rather than averaging opposing advice, and continue independent authorized work when a conflict blocks only another part. Maintain authority permits scoped upkeep of established choices; observe authority leaves proposed changes for the owner. Choose roots explicitly; the CLI does not infer which scope governs. The owner names the work; you maintain the practice's installed files only as that work and its grant warrant.
